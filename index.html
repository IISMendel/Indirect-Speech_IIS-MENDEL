A Introducing Reported Speech: say vs tell

SAY

  • Used when the person spoken to is NOT mentioned
  • Peter said, "Clare is still sleeping."
  • Peter said (that) Clare was still sleeping.
  • If a person IS mentioned → said to me (with preposition)

TELL

  • Used when the person spoken to IS mentioned
  • Peter told me (that) Clare was still sleeping.
  • NO preposition between tell and the person
  • With imperatives: tell + person + to
📌 Note: The conjunction that introducing the reported clause is optional and is often omitted in informal English. "He said (that) he was tired." is equally correct with or without that.
Other reporting verbs (backshift applies the same way): add, announce, assure, complain, confess, confirm, exclaim, explain, inform, promise, report, think

B Reporting Statements — Tense Backshift

When the reporting verb is in the past, the verb in the reported clause moves one step back into the past.

Direct Speech Tense Indirect Speech
"He is hungry." Present Simple She said he was hungry.
"Bill is sleeping." Present Continuous She said Bill was sleeping.
"I met him yesterday." Past Simple She said she had met him the day before.
"I was reading." Past Continuous She said she had been reading.
"She has received his letter." Present Perfect She said she had received his letter.
"I had been working all day." Past Perfect She said she had been working all day. ✓ no change
"I will see him tomorrow." Future (will) She said she would see him the following day.
"We are going to rain again." Going to She said it was going to rain again.
"He would see her." Present Conditional He said he would see her. ✓ no change
"He would have been driving to Liverpool." Past Conditional He said he would have been driving to Liverpool. ✓ no change

🗂 Changes in pronouns, time & place expressions

Pronouns

  • I, me → he / she, him / her
  • we, us → they, them
  • my, mine → his/her, his/hers
  • myself → himself / herself
  • this, these → that, those

Time & Place

  • now → then / at that moment
  • today → that day
  • tomorrow → the following day
  • yesterday → the previous day
  • ago → before
  • next week/year → the following week/year
  • last week/year → the previous week/year
  • here → there
⚠️ Timeless truths — the tense remains unchanged if the statement is always true: "Water begins to freeze at 0°C." → The teacher said that water begins to freeze at 0°C.
Future in the Past — when the original sentence already used would/was going to, it stays: "I'll help you." → He said he would help me. / "I'm leaving tomorrow." → He said he was leaving the following day.

C Reporting Questions

In reported questions the auxiliary do/does/did is dropped and the word order becomes affirmative (subject before verb). No question mark.

Yes / No Questions → if / whether

Direct "Are you tired?"→ He asked (me) if/whether I was tired.
Direct "Do you live here?"→ He asked (me) if/whether I lived there.

Wh- Questions → keep the question word

Direct "Where do you live?"→ He asked (me) where I lived.
Direct "What time did they leave?"→ He asked me what time they had left.

Reporting Answers (Short Answers)

Direct "Is she sleeping?" — "Yes, she is." → He asked if she was sleeping and I answered she was.

D Reporting Requests, Orders & Suggestions

Requests — ask / beg / request

ask / beg / request + person + (not) to + infinitive

"Can you lend me your dictionary?"→ He asked me to lend him his dictionary.
"Please don't hurt me!"→ She begged (me) not to hurt her.

Orders & Instructions — tell / command / instruct / forbid / warn

tell / command / instruct / forbid / warn + person + (not) to + infinitive

"Don't go near the river bank."→ Mrs Grey warned her children not to go near the river bank.

Suggestions — suggest / recommend / advise

suggest / recommend can take three constructions:

1. suggest + -ing → He suggested going to the pub.

2. suggest + (that) + should → He suggested (that) I should see a doctor.

3. suggest + (that) + frase → He recommended (that) I should see that film.

advise (when person NOT expressed): advise + -ing → The travel agent advised going somewhere else.

advise (when person IS expressed): advise + person + infinitive → The travel agent advised us to go somewhere else.

Other Reporting Verbs

Verb + to-infinitive

  • offer, promise, refuse
  • remind, threaten
  • agree, decide, invite

Verb + -ing form

  • accuse of, admit (to)
  • apologise for, complain about
  • deny, insist on, object to
  • regret, thank for
"I'm sorry I kept you waiting."→ He apologised for keeping me waiting.

E Modals in Indirect Speech

Direct Speech Indirect Speech Note
can could backshifts
may might backshifts
shall should backshifts
must had to backshifts
could, might, should, would, ought to, needn't unchanged ✓ invariant
"I can play the piano."→ He said he could play the piano.
"You must repair my car."→ He said he had to repair his car.
"You should keep off the grass."→ They said we should keep off the grass. (unchanged)

F Conditional Sentences, had better, would rather, wish

Type Direct Speech Indirect Speech
Type 0 "If you push this button, it starts." She said that if you push this button, it starts. (unchanged)
Type 1 "If she phones, I will be happy." She said that if she phoned, he would be happy.
Type 2 "If she phoned, I would be happy." He said that if she phoned, he would be happy. (unchanged)
Type 3 "If she had phoned, I would have been happy." He said that if she had phoned, he would have been happy. (unchanged)

had better / would rather → remain invariant in indirect speech.

"You'd better stay at home." → He said I'd better stay at home.

"I'd rather wake up early." → He said he'd rather wake up early.

wish → past simple and past perfect stay unchanged.

"I wish I were rich." → He said he wished he were rich.

"I wish I had seen her." → He said he wished he had seen her.
